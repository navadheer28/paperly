Interpretation
• After the data is collected and analysed using several data  analysis methods, the 
next task is to draw inferences from  these data.
• In other words, Interpretation of data needs to be done, so as  to derive certain 
conclusions, which is the whole purpose of  the research study.
• Interpretation can be broadly understood as the process of converting collected 
data into meaningful conclusions by :
• drawing inferences
• identifying patterns 
• making connections between different aspects of information gathered during 
the research to generate meaningful results

Need for Interpretation  ?? 
• Maintaining continuity of research
• Pointers for further research
• Communicate the significance of research
• Transition of exploratory research to experimental research
• It paves the way for the formulation of hypotheses for experimental research
• Through interpretation, the investigator can better understand why his /her 
findings are what they are
• It helps the researcher to make others to understand the real significance of his / her 
research findings
• It opens new avenues of intellectual venture and stimulates the hunt for more 
knowledge

Difference Between Analysis & Interpretation
• Analysis
helps in describing
 the general trends in thedata, and 
differences and similarities among data points
• Interpretation, on the other hand, relates data to the  objectives of the 
research. It explores relationships between  multiple measures, qualifies and 
amplifies data, draws  inferences and evaluates
• Analysis is made on the basis of observed data to identify the  pattern. This 
trend or pattern gives useful information about  the design or system
• Interpretation involves things like predicting the outcome,  observing the 
output response and associating meaning to  that output. In other words, 
making the numerical data  speak is the purpose of Interpretation

Bibliography 
• A systematic list of works, such as books and journal articles, that are related to 
a speciﬁc topic
• It can also be referred to as a list of works cited
• Bibliographies can be found at the end of books and articles or research paper 
• They can be organized by author, subject or date of publication
• The bibliography structure always includes citing the author's name, the title of 
the work, the year of publication and the publisher for each source, one consults 
for a paper or project
• Trillin, Calvin. “Culture Shopping.” New Yorker 15 Feb. 1993: 48 – 51
